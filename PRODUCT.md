# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

This site serves a mixed audience: recruiters evaluating Muhammad Samir Assawalhy's engineering experience, clients looking for a capable full-stack builder, open-source collaborators assessing projects and technical taste, and students or mentees looking for programming and career context.

Visitors arrive with different levels of time and technical depth. The site should let a recruiter scan quickly, while still giving curious readers enough narrative detail to understand the story behind the work, the engineering decisions, and the learning path.

## Product Purpose

The site presents Muhammad as a full-stack software engineer from Egypt with a strong background in web applications, competitive programming, open source, mentoring, and bilingual technical communication.

Success means the site feels like a living professional story rather than a static resume. Projects, experience, honors, writing, and contact paths should explain what happened, why it mattered, what was learned, and what kind of work Muhammad is ready to do next.

## Brand Personality

Technical, ambitious, curious.

The voice should be direct and specific, with enough explanation to show judgment and growth. It should avoid generic portfolio copy and should not reduce experience to short item lists. The site should feel like a guided walkthrough of real work, not a CV exported to the web.

## Anti-references

Do not make the site feel like just a resume. Avoid thin sections that only list roles, dates, tools, and links with little explanation.

Avoid generic developer portfolio templates, flashy startup landing-page tropes, and a purely dark hacker aesthetic. Animation should support the storytelling flow, especially on scroll, rather than becoming decoration detached from the content.

## Design Principles

1. Tell the work as a sequence of decisions, constraints, and outcomes.
2. Let technical credibility come from concrete details, not buzzwords.
3. Balance fast scanning with deeper reading paths for different audiences.
4. Treat bilingual English and Arabic support as a core part of the identity.
5. Use motion to pace the story and reveal relationships between sections.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use responsive layouts, keyboard-friendly navigation, readable contrast, RTL and LTR support, reduced-motion alternatives, and clear empty states. When there are no blog posts for a language or section, show a placeholder that indicates no blogs are available yet instead of leaving the area blank.
